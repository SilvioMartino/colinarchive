00 — Colin
Colin

Built this from nothing. I write because I have thoughts that don't fit anywhere else and build because nowhere else feels right either.

There is no going back to 2016 or 2006, but I dream of that world every time I see it back in a movie. There were times when you'd throw your phone into the trash instead of blocking someone and just go on with your day knowing you don't just not need that person, you don't need anything except yourself.

I made this place to commemorate those times — a public journal, blog, and GitHub where I don't care about anybody else's opinion and just share what I think on any subject. It can get unhinged, uncomfortable, unethical. It doesn't matter. Anyone who complains can make their own blog and post their hate there. Otherwise, you're a reader, not a critic.

01 — Archive
Archive

A personal publishing platform, built entirely from a vision that is still far to be achieved. Everything I write is meant to feel like it came from a person — not a product.

Essays are my thoughts in detail. Notes are my daily frustrations and ideas combined with shower thoughts. Code is my thoughts and ideas in technical form — or solutions to my problems.

Everything here gets updated. Revisiting a post might mean finding new material. The archive is alive in the same way a notebook is alive. It grows, it changes, it occasionally contradicts itself. That's by design, unwanted design, but it is beautiful in a unique way.

It was started months ago with the belief that even a single idea here could matter to the right person. That belief has not changed.

02 — The Rooms
Rooms

Three open chat rooms with currently no creeps. A throwback to the prime times when you could find friends on the internet and Discord was fun — not just morally broken people with an even more broken humor.

It's a place where you remember what the internet was supposed to be, and get a demo of that. With only you being the one to make it real.

03 — Commission
Commission

Seven dollars. You tell me the topic. One or two days later it's researched, written, and posted to the archive. I put your name on it if you want.

The money isn't the interesting part. The topic is. Give me something hard. Something you've been trying to figure out. Something that would take you three weeks to write yourself and still wouldn't come out right.

I've written about God and tits, about why we overthink ourselves into extinction, about the narrative problems in anime nobody talks about. The range is intentional. No off limits topics, only boring ones.

At the end, there's an actual piece of writing — not a summary or a bullet list. It lives in the archive for anyone to read.
